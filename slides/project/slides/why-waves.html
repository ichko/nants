Motivation

Why the waves, and not just x and y

  • The waves carry the same information as x and y.
  • A small network learns smooth functions easily and sharp ones very slowly.
  • The waves turn a smooth ramp into a distinct pattern at every cell, so the net can draw detail.

Tancik et al. (2020). Fourier Features Let Networks Learn High Frequency Functions in Low Dimensional Domains. NeurIPS 33.

The point in one line: the encoding, not the information, is what let it draw. Why a shallow relu net needs it: it fits smooth functions of its inputs quickly and sharp ones very slowly, and a 28-cell gecko is a sharp function of position; from a plain slope every update smears across the whole field, which is exactly what the failed pictures look like. The waves give each cell its own combination, so a change can stay local, and the four frequencies give coarse to fine: k=1 says which half of the field, k=8 resolves about three cells. Sine and cosine together remove the ambiguity of sine alone. The argument is from Tancik and colleagues, 2020: a small relu net has a spectral bias, it learns smooth functions first and sharp ones very slowly, and feeding the coordinates through sines and cosines at a ladder of frequencies moves the sharp detail into the range it learns easily. The biological cousin is the grid cells of the entorhinal cortex, a periodic code for position at several scales. Our own runs: no compass 0.13, raw x and y 0.10 after 2600 epochs, the full 35 numbers 0.0006.
